About

I recently completed my M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering at Gannon University, where I worked on the messy and exciting part where hardware meets autonomy. For two years, I worked with Prof. Wookwon Lee on a cyber-physical systems framework for a 1/5-scale autonomous vehicle.

My work involved designing and 3D-printing parts, troubleshooting wiring and hardware, integrating GPS and sensors, working with ROS and Linux, and testing the vehicle outdoors.I enjoy turning ideas into working systems and solving the problems that appear when hardware, software, and autonomy come together.

Research & projects

PROJECT 01 · FLAGSHIP

CPS Framework 1/5th-Scale Autonomous Vehicle

My two-year graduate project with Prof. Wookwon Lee: a full cyber-physical systems platform on an off-road chassis. I designed and 3D-printed the compute box and chassis parts, tracked down the PCB wiring faults that kept crashing it, integrated GPS and stereo cameras, and ran the vehicle through outdoor field tests on a ROS/Ubuntu stack. The platform became the testbed for our IEEE CCNC 2026 paper on sAV/sUAS video-based navigation.

PROJECT 02 · M.SC. PLAN C

Bidirectional DC-DC Converter for DC Microgrids

My M.Sc. Plan C project: a droop-controlled converter balancing power between solar, battery, and load. It held the bus voltage steady at about 98% as loads changed, and tuning the MPPT pushed energy harvest up 65% in MATLAB/Simulink simulation, with state-of-charge (SoC) balancing layered over the voltage and current controllers.

PROJECT 05

Zumo Bot: Autonomous Path Follower

Programmed a Zumo 32U4 robot to drive a taped path on its own, reading a 6-channel IR reflectance array to decide when to go straight, turn, or stop. I wrote the ESP32/Arduino logic for sensor thresholds, turn decisions, and end-of-path detection and tuned speed and turn sensitivity through repeated testing.
